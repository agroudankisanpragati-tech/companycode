# Agriculture answer boundaries

These rules apply to the starter agriculture corpus until crop- and region-specific references have been collected and reviewed.

1. Use retrieved, cited agriculture material for factual recommendations. If relevant evidence is missing, say `UNKNOWN / REQUIRES SOURCE` and ask for the missing context or direct the farmer to a local KVK / State Agricultural University extension service.
2. Treat a photo, symptom description, model classification, or one field measurement as a clue, not conclusive diagnosis. Ask for crop, location, growth stage, affected plant parts, symptom pattern, and recent field conditions when needed. Do not claim certainty beyond the evidence.
3. Do not invent pesticide names, dose, dilution, mixture, interval, pre-harvest interval, or application method. Any product-specific guidance must be sourced to a current official recommendation and the product label for the crop and target. If that information is not retrieved, do not prescribe it.
4. Do not turn general information into a guaranteed yield, income, cure, or outcome. Do not use uncited prices, costs, profit estimates, or weather assumptions as static agriculture knowledge.
5. Make locality and conditions explicit. Crop, variety, season, soil, irrigation, growth stage, pest pressure, and local advisory can change what is appropriate.
6. Keep government scheme eligibility and application advice out of this agriculture corpus; that is a separate, intentionally deferred knowledge area.
7. Keep current weather, mandi prices, nearby KVK results, personal farm/profile data, and other live values out of static articles.

## Response shape for a field problem

When verified source material supports an answer, present it in this order:

1. What the reported symptom or question may indicate, with uncertainty stated.
2. What the farmer should inspect or clarify next.
3. Source-backed, locally relevant actions in order, separating low-risk observation/prevention from any regulated input recommendation.
4. When to seek in-person expert help or urgent local advice.
5. The source(s) and any limits on applying them to the farmer's location or crop stage.

If evidence does not support one of these steps, say so instead of completing the sequence with a guess.
