# Integrated Pest Management (IPM): general principles

ICAR describes Integrated Pest Management as combining appropriate methods to keep pest populations below economically damaging levels while reducing risks to people and the environment. The approach emphasizes prevention, regular monitoring, informed decisions, and priority for non-chemical measures. The suitable combination depends on the crop, pest, and field conditions.

## General decision sequence

1. **Observe and identify.** Check the affected crop and plant parts, the extent and pattern of damage, and whether the suspected organism is actually present. A symptom alone may not identify a pest or disease.
2. **Monitor and assess.** Record how much of the crop is affected and whether the problem is changing. Use crop- and pest-specific local thresholds or advisories when available; this general article does not provide thresholds.
3. **Consider prevention and non-chemical methods first.** ICAR lists cultural, physical, mechanical, varietal, biological, bio-rational, and biotechnological methods among the options that may be combined, depending on the situation.
4. **Use chemical control only when necessary and supported by a current recommendation.** Follow the official crop/target recommendation and the product label. This article does not recommend a pesticide, dose, mixture, or spray schedule.
5. **Recheck and record.** Monitor the field after taking action and seek local extension advice if damage continues, identification is uncertain, or a high-value crop is at risk.

This sequence is a general way to think through IPM; it is not a crop-specific treatment plan. Do not assume that the same method is appropriate in every region or for every pest.
