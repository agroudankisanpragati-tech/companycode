# Using a soil-test report

A soil-test report describes measurements from the sample that was submitted. Depending on the report, it may include soil reaction (pH), electrical conductivity, organic carbon, major nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium, and other nutrients. The Indian Soil Health Card FAQ describes nutrient status and fertilizer/soil-amendment recommendations based on analyzed soil results. The report is most useful when read with the crop and field context for which it was prepared.

## What to do

1. Check that the report belongs to the correct field and note its sampling date and the laboratory that issued it.
2. Read the reported measurements and units as printed. Do not compare values from different methods or units as if they were directly interchangeable.
3. Follow the crop-specific recommendation from the report or ask the issuing laboratory / local agricultural extension expert to explain it. Do not calculate a fertilizer dose from a single nutrient value without the crop, soil, units, and local recommendation.
4. If the report is old, incomplete, or seems inconsistent with the field, ask the soil-testing laboratory or local extension service whether a new representative sample is needed.

## Limits

This is general guidance for reading a report, not a fertilizer prescription or interpretation of a particular sample. The appropriate interpretation and input plan depend on the crop, field, sampling and laboratory method, local recommendations, and the report itself. No numerical threshold or dose is supplied in this article.
